The story of the attention mechanism

1. The bottleneck

Early sequence-to-sequence models squeezed a whole sentence into one fixed-length vector. Long sentences lost information, and translation quality fell as sentences grew longer.

Bahdanau, Cho and Bengio proposed in 2014 that the decoder should look back at every encoder state and weight them by relevance at each step.

2. Scoring relevance

Each decoder step scores every encoder state, turns the scores into weights with a softmax, and takes the weighted sum as its context vector.

The weights are learned end to end: no one tells the model which words to align, yet the learned alignments often match human intuition about translation.

3. The plot twist

In 2017 the Transformer removed recurrence entirely and kept only attention, stacking self-attention layers so every token attends to every other token in parallel.

The original Transformer was trained on the WMT 2014 English-to-German and English-to-French translation tasks, using eight P100 GPUs.
